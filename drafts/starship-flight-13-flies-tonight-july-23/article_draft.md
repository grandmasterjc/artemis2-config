Starship Flight 13 is back on the pad. After last Thursday's ignition abort — four Raptors that never lit, two of them since removed and replaced — and a stand-down from Monday's window, SpaceX is now targeting tonight, Thursday, July 23, at 22:45 UTC (6:45 p.m. EDT) from Starbase. The launch window runs 90 minutes.

The scorecard is unchanged, and worth keeping at hand during the webcast:

- Does Super Heavy finally stick its controlled splashdown in the Gulf? It missed on Flight 12.
- Does Ship repeat its on-target arrival off Western Australia, and does the in-space Raptor relight work this time?
- Do the first 20 Starlink V3 satellites deploy cleanly on the suborbital arc? Six of them carry cameras aimed back at Ship's heat shield during reentry — a flying inspection of the vehicle's most stubborn component.

For Artemis, the stakes are the same as they were last week: this is the vehicle family NASA needs flying often before the propellant-transfer demonstration later this year, the test the Artemis IV lunar landing actually depends on. A clean flight tonight puts the cadence argument back on track. A third stand-down would start to be a story of its own.
