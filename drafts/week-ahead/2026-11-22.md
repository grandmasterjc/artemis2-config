# The Week Ahead: November 23 to 29, 2026

A short briefing on what to watch in human lunar exploration and the surrounding launch market over the next seven days.

---

## Vulcan fell short of orbit on its return to flight

Last Sunday's edition went out with Vulcan on the pad. It lifted off from SLC-41 at 17:40 UTC on November 15 and did not reach orbit. All 40 Amazon Leo satellites were lost, and [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/11/15/ula-suffers-first-total-mission-failure-with-loss-of-vulcan-rocket-carrying-40-amazon-leo-satellites/) describes it as the first outright mission failure in United Launch Alliance's history.

ULA's account has sharpened over the week. Its statements, as quoted by [The Register](https://theregister.com/science/2026/11/18/ula-says-vulcan-lacked-the-oomph-to-get-amazon-satellites-to-orbit/5310712), say the rocket "experienced low booster performance" just after liftoff, so the stages separated too low and too slow for the Centaur upper stage to make up the difference. A later update said the low thrust was probably "the result of inadequate fuel supply" to the two BE-4 engines, and that the solid rocket boosters, the cause of the earlier grounding, performed nominally. Both findings are preliminary.

The FAA is requiring a mishap investigation, according to [SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/). ULA has given no date for a next flight.

## Why a Vulcan failure reaches Artemis IV

The stage that was not blamed is the one NASA cares about. On October 23, [ULA announced](https://newsroom.ulalaunch.com/releases/united-launch-alliance-is-collaborating-with-nasa-for-the-development-of-a-centaur-upper-stage-for-artemis-missions) it is working with NASA on a Centaur upper stage for SLS on future Artemis missions, the replacement for the cancelled Exploration Upper Stage. If the fuel supply finding holds, the fault sits in the first stage, which SLS does not share. I found no NASA statement on whether the investigation changes anything for Artemis IV.

## Amazon flies again on Monday, on Ariane 64

[Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/project-kuiper-satellite-rocket-launch-progress-updates) lists its next launch, LE-04, for Monday, November 23 at 11:09 UTC from Kourou, with 36 satellites on an Ariane 64.

## Starship Flight 16 still has no date

SpaceX has not announced Flight 16. Secondary reporting points to December at the earliest, possibly 2027, and suggests it may be the first Starship launch from Kennedy's Pad 39A. Reports on Flight 15 now tie the lost booster to a low propellant reading that cut its boostback burn short, with the root cause still open. I again found no FAA statement on whether a mishap investigation is required, and no date for the propellant transfer test the lunar lander depends on.

## Senate appropriators propose $24.9 billion for NASA

The Senate's Commerce-Justice-Science subcommittee released its FY2027 bill on November 12 with $24.874 billion for NASA, [SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/senate-appropriations-cjs-subcommittee-proposes-24-9-billion-for-nasa-in-fy2027/) reports, against an administration request of $18.8 billion. No full committee markup is scheduled, no FY2027 bill has been enacted, and the continuing resolution still expires on December 11.

On November 18 the Senate Commerce Committee advanced nominees for NASA chief financial officer and inspector general.

## An RS-25 firing at Stennis, and nothing new from the VAB

NASA test-fired development engine 10001 for 200 seconds at Stennis on November 18, part of work on the RS-25 engines for later SLS flights. NASA has still given no date for rolling the Artemis III core stage and boosters to Pad 39B for the tanking test.

Blue Origin: I found nothing new this week. New Glenn's return remains reported for January 2027.

## The firmest and softest dates this week

The Ariane 64 launch has a time published by the customer and is the firmest entry. The Falcon 9 flights are marked go, though two of the three have already slipped. Shenzhou 24, Spectrum and Kinetica 1 are the ones to hold loosely.
