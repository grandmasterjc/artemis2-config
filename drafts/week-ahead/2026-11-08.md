# The Week Ahead: November 9 to 15, 2026

A short briefing on what to watch in human lunar exploration and the surrounding launch market over the next seven days.

---

## New Glenn is now expected in January, and Blue Moon waits with it

The biggest Artemis-adjacent news of the past week is a schedule slip. On November 5, [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/blue-origin-new-glenn-return-to-flight-delayed-january) reported, citing Bloomberg and the Financial Times, that Blue Origin CEO Dave Limp told staff New Glenn will return to flight in January 2027. The company had previously aimed to fly again before the end of 2026. New Glenn has been grounded since it exploded during a static fire at its only pad on May 28. Blue Origin declined to comment.

The slip matters for Artemis because New Glenn is the rocket meant to carry Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander. Reporting puts the Blue Moon Mark 1 Pathfinder at no earlier than the first quarter of 2027, and the revised Artemis III plan, as described in secondary coverage last month, also needs a Blue Moon test article in orbit before Orion launches.

## Vulcan's return to flight is set for Friday

United Launch Alliance has announced Friday, November 13 for Vulcan's first launch in nine months, according to [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/10/28/united-launch-alliance-announces-mid-november-return-to-flight-for-vulcan-rocket/). The flight, Amazon Leo LV-01, carries 40 satellites from Cape Canaveral SLC-41. Vulcan has been grounded since nozzle failures on its Northrop Grumman solid rocket boosters, the most recent on the February USSF-87 mission.

Amazon lists liftoff at 12:54 p.m. Eastern, which is 17:54 UTC, pending range approval. [Space Right Now](https://spacerightnow.com/launches) and [Orbital Radar](https://orbitalradar.com/blog/orbital-briefing-2026-11-08) both carry that time, and Orbital Radar notes it is subject to change. Space Force leadership has said it will not clear Vulcan for national security payloads until it has data from commercial flights.

## Starship Flight 15 has a provisional slot, but no license update

Space Right Now and Orbital Radar both list Starship Flight 15 for Friday, November 13 at 22:45 UTC from Starbase Pad 2, and both mark it unconfirmed. SpaceX has not published a date. The hardware looks ready on the evidence available: Booster 22 completed a 33-engine static fire on October 25, and SpaceX announced on October 28 that Ship 42 had finished two static fires. Elon Musk said on the Tesla earnings call that both stages will attempt a catch.

The open item is regulatory. One report says SpaceX still needs a modified FAA license allowing both stages to return over land to the launch site, and I could not find that the FAA has issued it. A published license would be the clearest signal that November 13 is real. Starship dates have moved repeatedly.

## Launches, in UTC

Times are from [Space Right Now](https://spacerightnow.com/launches) and [Orbital Radar](https://orbitalradar.com/blog/orbital-briefing-2026-11-08). Where only one source carries an entry, that is stated.

- Falcon 9 / USSF-627: Tuesday, November 10, 03:03 UTC, from Vandenberg SLC-4E. Both sources agree and mark it go.
- Mir demo flight: Wednesday, November 11, around 05:00 UTC, from a sea launch platform. Only Space Right Now lists it.
- Long March 2D: Wednesday, November 11, 11:40 UTC, from Jiuquan, payload unannounced. Single source, and Chinese times often change without notice.
- Falcon 9 / USSF-317: Friday, November 13, 04:23 UTC, from Vandenberg SLC-4E. Listed by Space Right Now only among the sources checked.
- Vulcan / Amazon Leo LV-01: Friday, November 13, 17:54 UTC, as above.
- Starship Flight 15: Friday, November 13, 22:45 UTC, provisional, as above.
- Electron / The Mountain God Rises (iQPS): Saturday, November 14, 05:30 UTC, from Mahia, New Zealand. One listing is on a page that looks stale, so the date may move.

Starlink Group 15-21, which last week's edition listed for early November, now shows as undated, with one tracker giving November 30.

## SLS and Orion have had no public update

NASA has posted no new Artemis III hardware news since the October 23 briefing. The next visible milestone on the ground is joining the core stage to the stacked boosters in the Vehicle Assembly Building, followed by a tanking test at Pad 39B that reporting places around the end of 2026 without a date. I found nothing to suggest either happens this week.

## Moon Base and budget: nothing new to report

The latest NASA Moon Base item I could verify is the [June 30 release](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-more-moon-base-science-previews-new-opportunities/), which awarded nearly $600 million for four CLPS deliveries in late 2028: Astrobotic $297.9 million for two, Intuitive Machines $148.3 million, and Firefly Aerospace $144.2 million. I found no new awards this week.

On money, I could not find the current FY2027 appropriations status or any new inspector general report on Artemis, so I am not characterising either.

## How firm each date is

Vulcan has an official date and a published time, but a first flight after a nine-month grounding can slip. The Falcon 9 flights are the likeliest to go on time. Starship, the Mir demo, the Long March and Electron entries rest on one or two trackers and should be held loosely.
