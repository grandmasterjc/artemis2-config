# The Week Ahead: November 16 to 22, 2026

A short briefing on what to watch in human lunar exploration and the surrounding launch market over the next seven days.

---

## Ship 42 returned from orbit and was caught at Starbase

Starship Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase Pad 2 on Friday, November 13 at 22:47 UTC. The FAA issued the modified license covering the return of both stages on launch day, about three hours before liftoff according to [secondary reporting](https://www.teslaoracle.com/2026/11/13/spacex-is-still-waiting-for-regulatory-approval-hours-ahead-of-flight-15-launch/).

Ship 42 reached low Earth orbit, deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, completed about 14 orbits and was caught by the tower arms at Pad 2 on Saturday, roughly 22 and a half hours after launch. [RTE](https://www.rte.ie/news/world/2026/1115/1599250-spacex-us-world/) and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/science/space/spacex-launches-15th-starship-flight-aborts-booster-recovery-maneuver-rcna609274) both report it as the first time an upper stage has been caught after returning from orbit. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman called the recovery "almost magic."

The booster half of the double catch did not happen. Booster 22 aborted its return to the tower, diverted to the Gulf of Mexico and was destroyed. Accounts of the cause differ: some cite a guidance issue, others low propellant after the boostback burn, and [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-says-starship-flight-15-booster-aborted-chopsticks-landing-catch-after-launch-due-to-guidance-issue-video) reports SpaceX is still investigating. I found no FAA statement on whether a mishap investigation is required.

## What the catch does not settle for the lunar lander

A caught ship is a reuse milestone. It is not the test the Starship lander depends on, which is transferring cryogenic propellant between two ships in orbit. Flight 15 did not attempt it, and SpaceX has not said which flight will. Secondary coverage of NASA's October briefing places that demonstration in early 2027, which I could not confirm against a NASA document. SpaceX has not announced a date for Flight 16.

## Vulcan's Amazon Leo launch has moved twice, to Sunday

Vulcan did not fly on Friday. ULA stood down after a power interruption at an Air Liquide gaseous nitrogen plant cut supply to SLC-41, and said the rocket and its 40 satellites were healthy, according to [New Space Economy](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/11/14/vulcan-amazon-leo-ground-supply-delay/). A Saturday attempt was listed and then replaced without a published reason.

[ULA's mission page](https://www.ulalaunch.com/missions/next-launch/vulcan-leo-1) now gives Sunday, November 15, with a window from 17:11 to 17:40 UTC. That is today, so the launch may have flown or slipped again by the time you read this.

## Electron reached 100 launches

Rocket Lab's The Mountain God Rises flew on Saturday, November 14 at 05:30 UTC from Mahia, as listed here last week, and delivered the QPS-SAR-19 radar satellite for iQPS. [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/rocket-lab-100th-launch-iqps-electron-sar-satellite) reports it as the company's 100th launch.

## A correction on the Artemis III core stage

Last week's edition said NASA had posted no Artemis III hardware news since October 23 and that joining the core stage to the boosters was still ahead. That was wrong. [NASA reported on October 28](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/artemis-iii-sls-core-stage-joins-boosters-at-kennedy/) that the 212-foot core stage had been lifted onto the mobile launcher between the two boosters in the Vehicle Assembly Building's High Bay 3.

The next step is rolling that stack to Pad 39B for a tanking test that loads liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen into the core stage only. NASA gives no date for the rollout or the test, and I found nothing new from NASA this week.

## NASA is funded at last year's level until December 11

NASA is operating under a continuing resolution that runs to December 11, as [SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/house-clears-fy2027-cr-now-to-the-president/) reported when it passed. Reporting since the midterms describes appropriators as far apart on full-year FY2027 bills, which makes another stopgap the likelier outcome. I found no new inspector general report on Artemis and no NASA hearing scheduled for this week.

## Blue Origin: no change to the January target

New Glenn's return to flight remains reported for January 2027. The only new item I found is a third stage called GS3, which Blue Origin unveiled on November 5 according to NASASpaceFlight. It uses the BE-7 engine from the Blue Moon landers, and Blue Origin says the stage is not required for its lander architecture.

## Which dates are firm

The two Falcon 9 flights are marked go and are the likeliest to fly on time. The Long March 8A entry is the softest. Vulcan has an official window today but has already moved twice in three days.
