# The Week Ahead: October 19 to 25, 2026

A short briefing on what to watch in human lunar exploration and the surrounding launch market over the next seven days.

---

## Friday's Artemis briefing finally has a date

The update Jared Isaacman promised for "early October" now has a time. NASA's [media advisory M26-081](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-discuss-artemis-acceleration-status-of-upcoming-moon-missions/), issued October 14, sets a briefing for 2 p.m. EDT on Friday, October 23, which is 18:00 UTC. Isaacman, Associate Administrator Amit Kshatriya and Artemis program manager Jeremy Parsons will take part, and it streams at nasa.gov/live.

NASA says the briefing will cover Artemis III, which will send four astronauts to low Earth orbit next year to test systems needed for lunar landings, and progress toward returning astronauts to the Moon by 2028. The advisory's text places the event at Kennedy Space Center, while the page's location field says NASA Headquarters. 

What to listen for is specific. Isaacman has floated launching the Space Launch System roughly every ten months instead of every two to three years, and he has described south pole landers on a monthly cadence from 2027. Neither has a schedule attached yet. A published date for the Core Stage-3 lift onto the mobile launcher would also be a concrete sign of progress. As of Sunday, the last public hardware update was the September 30 completion of Artemis III booster stacking in the Vehicle Assembly Building, with all four RS-25 engines already on the core stage.

## What launches, in UTC

Times below come from [Space Launch Schedule](https://www.spacelaunchschedule.com/) and the [Wikipedia launch list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_spaceflight_launches_in_October%E2%80%93December_2026), which agree on every entry.

- Falcon 9 / Bandwagon-5: Monday, October 19, 10:30 UTC (6:30 a.m. EDT), from Cape Canaveral SLC-40. A dedicated mid-inclination rideshare with dozens of small satellites for commercial and government customers.
- H3 / Martian Moons eXploration (MMX): Monday, October 19, 19:41 UTC (04:41 on October 20 in Japan), from Tanegashima. JAXA's probe will orbit Mars, study its two moons and collect samples from Phobos, and it carries the Idefix rover built by DLR and CNES.
- Falcon 9 / Starlink Group 15-15: Tuesday, October 20, 22:14 UTC, from Vandenberg SLC-4E.
- Electron / HawkEye 360: Wednesday, October 21, 05:00 UTC, from Mahia, New Zealand, with three radio-frequency mapping satellites. Space Launch Schedule flags this one as subject to change.
- Chinese launches: Ceres-1S on October 21 around 03:35 UTC, Long March 8A on October 22 around 02:57 UTC, and Long March 6A and Long March 4B on October 23 at about 10:15 and 19:25 UTC. All four carry unannounced payloads, and Chinese dates often shift without explanation, so treat them as soft.
- Falcon 9 / Starlink Group 15-16: Saturday, October 24, 05:29 UTC, from Vandenberg, with 27 satellites.

Several other missions, including Ariane 6 and a GSLV, carry only "October" with no day, so I have left them out.

## ISS cargo is at the station, with solar arrays for the aging truss

The station story from last week has moved on. Cargo Dragon CRS-35 launched on October 13 at 10:33 UTC and, per [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/?p=74515), was due to dock at Harmony's zenith port on October 15 at about 15:00 UTC. A single post-event report says it did, but I could not find a NASA confirmation, so treat the docking as expected rather than verified. The trunk holds the final pair of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays, and Dragon is planned to stay about a month.

## Starship Flight 15 is now a late November question

Flight 14 reached orbit on September 28. Reporting this week quotes Elon Musk putting Flight 15, which is expected to attempt a ship catch, four to six weeks out, which points to shortly before Thanksgiving. An earlier October 19 date from air-traffic planning documents should be considered dead. These sources are secondary, and SpaceX has not published a date or confirmed the recovery plan. Ship 42 and Booster 22 have finished cryogenic testing, with static fires still ahead. If a static fire appears this week, that is the real schedule signal.

## Blue Moon still waits on New Glenn

Blue Origin has said it will return to flight by the end of 2026 after the May 28 static-fire explosion destroyed a booster and upper stage. The pad damage and the investigation pushed the first Blue Moon Mark 1 launch to early 2027, and NASA has looked at whether the intact lander could fly on another rocket. I found no new Blue Origin announcement this week. Anything Isaacman says on Friday about commercial lander readiness will be the first official read on how that affects the Artemis III schedule.

## Dates that could still move

The Friday briefing time is set by NASA, though the venue is inconsistent in the advisory. MMX and Bandwagon-5 depend on weather and range readiness. The Electron launch and all four Chinese launches are the least firm entries. Flight 15 has no official date.
