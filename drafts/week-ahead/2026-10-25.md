# The Week Ahead: October 26 to November 1, 2026

A short briefing on what to watch in human lunar exploration and the surrounding launch market over the next seven days.

---

## Artemis III becomes a rendezvous test in low Earth orbit

The briefing promised for weeks took place on Friday, October 23, at Kennedy Space Center. NASA's [account of the event](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/10/23/nasa-shares-artemis-acceleration-plans-for-moon-missions/) is brief. It says Artemis III will send four astronauts to low Earth orbit in 2027 to test systems needed for lunar landings, that Artemis IV is the first mission planned for the lunar surface, and that the 2028 landing goal is "progressing." Administrator Jared Isaacman, Associate Administrator Amit Kshatriya and program manager Jeremy Parsons spoke.

The mission details come mostly from secondary reporting, so read them as likely rather than final. [New Space Economy](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/10/24/nasa-targets-summer-2027-artemis-iii-launch-and-year-end-sls-tanking-test/) describes a flight of about 14 days with this sequence: Blue Origin launches a Blue Moon Mark 2 test article into a circular orbit near 273 miles, SLS launches Orion several days later, the crew docks with Blue Moon for about two days, then Orion docks with a Starship Version 3 test vehicle for up to two hours. Astronauts do not enter Starship. Splashdown is in the Pacific. The same report names the crew as Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, Andre Douglas and ESA's Luca Parmitano, and says a spacer replaces the upper stage. I could not match the crew names or the spacer to a NASA page, and outlets differ on whether the launch lands in summer or mid-2027.

## What the SLS hardware needs before any of that

NASA's own hardware status has not changed since the end of September. Twin boosters were stacked on the mobile launcher in the Vehicle Assembly Building on September 30, and all four RS-25 engines are on the core stage. The next step is joining core stage and boosters. That short stack is then due to roll to Pad 39B for a propellant-loading test without Orion, which reporting places at the end of 2026 with no exact date. A lift of the core stage onto the mobile launcher this week would be the first visible sign that the year-end target is holding.

## A manifest dominated by Starlink

Times are UTC. Where trackers disagree, the disagreement is stated.

- Long March 8A: Sunday, October 25, around 01:50 UTC, from Wenchang, payload unidentified, per [Orbital Radar](https://orbitalradar.com/blog/orbital-briefing-2026-10-24). This falls just before the window.
- Falcon 9 / Starlink Group 15-16: Tuesday, October 27, 04:45 UTC, from Vandenberg. Last week's edition listed this mission for October 24, so it has slipped by three days. Orbital Radar marks the time as subject to change.
- Falcon 9 / Starlink Group 15-18: Orbital Radar gives Friday, October 30, 04:02 UTC, while KeepTrack gives Wednesday, October 28, 04:31 UTC. I would trust neither until a launch day is announced.
- Vulcan / Amazon Leo LV-01: listed for Thursday, October 29 from Cape Canaveral SLC-41, with no launch time. [Wikipedia's launch list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_spaceflight_launches_in_October%E2%80%93December_2026) and NextSpaceflight both carry the date, but a tracker snapshot from mid-October still showed the status as unconfirmed. A slip is plausible.
- Long March 12A, second flight: Saturday, October 31, time not given, from one tracker whose page looks stale. Treat as unconfirmed.

An ISRO navigation launch showed up in one listing for October 30, but I found no confirming source and have left it out.

## Blue Origin has nothing new to report

New Glenn has not flown since the May 28 pad explosion. Blue Origin's Blue Moon Mark 1 Endurance, which NASA contracted to deliver payloads to Shackleton Connecting Ridge, was due to launch this fall and has moved to early 2027. The Artemis III plan now puts a different Blue Moon vehicle on New Glenn's critical path, so the return to flight matters more than before. NASA's [July agreement](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-support-blue-origin-new-glenn-rocket-testing-advance-artemis/) with Blue Origin schedules second-stage hot-fire testing at Stennis Space Center's B-2 stand for "fall 2026." I found no report that it has begun. A first hot fire there would be the most concrete evidence of progress this week.

## Starship Flight 15 still has no published date

SpaceX has not announced a date for Flight 15, which is expected to attempt a ship catch. Last week's edition cited a late-November expectation from secondary reporting, and I found nothing since that confirms or contradicts it. The Artemis III profile assumes a Starship Version 3 vehicle will be ready to launch from Florida and dock with Orion in orbit, and SpaceX has said a large-scale propellant transfer demonstration is planned for early 2027. A static fire on the Flight 15 booster or ship would be the first real schedule signal.

## Dates that could still move

Every launch above except the Starlink flight on October 27 is soft, with conflicting or thin sourcing. The Artemis III timeline is a target stated in the press, not a baselined schedule, and the crew list and hardware details await a NASA publication. I did not find new budget or inspector general news affecting Artemis this week.
