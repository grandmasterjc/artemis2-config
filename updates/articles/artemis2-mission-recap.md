After ten days in deep space, four astronauts are safely back on Earth. Artemis II delivered what NASA set out to prove: that the Space Launch System, Orion, and the European Service Module can safely carry humans beyond low Earth orbit and back. Here's a visual recap of the mission that took humans farther from Earth than anyone since Apollo 17.

## The Launch

Liftoff from Pad 39B on April 1, 2026 at 18:35 EDT.

![Artemis II on the pad](https://grandmasterjc.github.io/artemis2-config/updates/images/artemis2_mission.jpg)

The SLS Block 1 rocket produced 8.8 million pounds of thrust at ignition — the most powerful rocket to carry humans since the Saturn V.

## Lunar Flyby

On flight day 6, Orion reached its closest approach to the Moon — just 9,200 km above the far side. From there, the crew could see the Earth rise over the lunar horizon.

![Earth rise from lunar orbit](https://grandmasterjc.github.io/artemis2-config/updates/images/artemis2_photos.jpg)

Commander Reid Wiseman photographed the view during flight day 6. No human had seen this perspective in over 50 years.

## Splashdown

The Orion capsule splashed down in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of San Diego on April 11, 2026 at 00:07 UTC. All four crew members were recovered in good health within hours.
