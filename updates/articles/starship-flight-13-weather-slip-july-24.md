Starship stayed on the ground Thursday after all. About two hours before the window opened, SpaceX pushed Flight 13 to tonight — Friday, July 24, at 22:45 UTC (6:45 p.m. EDT) — citing weather at Starbase.

The reason is more interesting than a generic scrub. "A key objective for the flight test is to get clear imagery from the ground of Starship's heatshield as it flies at a higher dynamic pressure during ascent, which won't be possible with today's weather conditions," the company wrote, adding that visibility is forecast to be ideal for tonight.

In other words: the heat shield — the component this program has wrestled with longest — is enough of a priority on this flight that SpaceX would rather lose a day than lose the pictures. Between the ground cameras during ascent and the six Starlink V3 satellites that will film the shield from space during reentry, Flight 13 is instrumented around that one component from both ends.

The rest of the scorecard is unchanged from our earlier briefings: Super Heavy's controlled Gulf splashdown (missed on Flight 12), Ship's arrival off Western Australia, the in-space Raptor relight, and the first deployment of 20 Starlink V3 satellites.
